Product Representation Controls

Defining the foundations for flexible product representation

Key contributions

  • Recognised that Configure Sources and Product Data Versions were two expressions of the same advertiser need: intentional control over how products appear to buyers.
  • Helped define Product Representation Controls as the shared product direction across both investments, connecting one consistent product representation and flexible representations under one foundation.
  • Partnered with engineering to define foundational behaviours before build began, clarifying what Product Data Versions should and shouldn’t control so the product evolved around advertiser needs rather than internal assumptions.
  • Developed the design vision for Product Data Versions, aligning teams around how product representation could evolve and shaping the roadmap for 5+ downstream workstreams.
  • Translated alpha learnings into beta priorities, use-case framing, and setup guidance for advertisers.

Role

Lead Product Designer

Team

1 CD, 11 Eng, 1UXR, PM and PMM consult

Timeframe

11 months

Meta's product catalog ↗ powers the product information buyers see across ads and commerce surfaces. For most advertisers, one catalogue was enough. More sophisticated advertisers had two different representation problems.

Some needed one reliable product representation when multiple sources provided conflicting titles, images, prices, or availability. Others needed the same products to appear differently across objectives, markets, or surfaces.

These were initially scoped as separate investments: Configure Sources and Product Data Versions. As I got closer to both problem spaces, I realised they were two expressions of the same underlying need: advertisers wanted more intentional control over how products appeared to buyers.

That insight became the foundation for Product Representation Controls: a shared product direction for how advertisers manage product representation across Meta.

Two capabilities, one foundation

Configure Sources

Helped advertisers create one consistent product representation by showing which data source was winning for each attribute, and giving them controls to change it.

Product Data Versions

Helped advertisers adapt that representation for different objectives, markets, or surfaces without maintaining separate catalogs.

Together, they became one foundation for how advertisers control product representation across Meta.

Design principles

Configure Sources established the first control pattern. As the work expanded into Product Data Versions, the challenge was making sure flexible representation built on that pattern instead of introducing a separate way to manage product data.

Transparency before control

Advertisers needed to understand their data before they could confidently change it. Configure Sources showed which source was winning for each attribute before offering controls to change it. Product Data Versions let advertisers validate version data before anything went live.

Build on what advertisers already know

Advertisers should not have to relearn how product control works every time a new type of control is added. Product Data Versions reused the prioritisation patterns introduced in Configure Sources, so flexible representation built on controls advertisers already understood.

Build foundations that can evolve

Localisation was not part of the beta, but the underlying structure had to support it later. Making those decisions early meant future capabilities could extend the foundation instead of forcing advertisers to relearn how product representation worked.

Configure Sources

Configure Sources addressed the first representation problem: when multiple sources provided conflicting product information, advertisers needed to understand which source was winning, why, and how to change it.

The key design challenge was sequencing: advertisers needed to understand why the system was behaving the way it was before they felt confident changing it. That principle shaped the final experience.

Surfacing conflicts before offering controls

Advertisers could not fix what they could not see. Attributes where sources disagreed were surfaced first, ordered by business consequence. From there, advertisers could drag to reprioritise which source should win for each attribute.

Making the default visible

Meta had always selected a winning source when conflicts existed, but advertisers could not see which source was selected or why. The new experience made that choice visible, so advertisers could make an informed decision about their data. They could override it, but at least one source always had to be set, ensuring buyers continued to see accurate product information.

Results

By making prioritisation transparent before making it configurable, the catalog stopped feeling like something that happened to advertisers and became something they could actively control.

  • Exceeded the revenue protection goal by 2x, protecting hundreds of millions in advertiser revenue.
  • Unblocked advertisers who couldn't run product ads without source-level control.
  • Saved 2.66 engineering years previously spent manually resolving data conflicts.
  • Established the foundation for Product Data Versions and future objective-specific catalog customisation within a single catalog

Product Data Versions

Product Data Versions addressed the second representation problem: advertisers needed to adapt the same product for different markets, surfaces, and marketing objectives.

The key decision was defining what a Version should control. Advertisers needed to change context-specific product information, like titles, images, descriptions, or other attributes, without duplicating products or maintaining separate catalogs. That meant a Version should change how product information appears in a specific context, without changing the default product information used elsewhere.

Creating a Version

When creating a Version, advertisers could choose from data sources already in their catalog, or upload a new source for that Version only. This let them introduce alternate product information without changing the default product information buyers saw elsewhere.

When multiple sources were selected, advertisers could drag them into priority order using the same pattern introduced in Configure Sources. For the beta, we kept this control at the source level, so advertisers could prioritise a curated Version source while using existing sources to fill in the rest.

Validate products before running ads

More flexibility meant advertisers needed clearer ways to trust the output before using a Version in ads. They needed to confirm that Version data had applied correctly and ads would appear as intended before committing spend.

Instead of relying on one preview to explain the whole system, we built validation into the moments where confidence mattered: after data upload, when products were organised for ads, and during ad setup.

Results

Product Data Versions validated demand for flexible product representation and gave the team a foundation for future representation capabilities.

  • Generated millions in incremental campaign revenue within the first two months of beta.
  • Beta advertisers reported consistent ROAS improvement1.
  • Averaged 8.5/10 satisfaction across exit interviews.
  • Design vision shaped the roadmap for 5+ downstream product representation workstreams.
Key Takeaway

In technical product spaces, the interface is only as clear as the assumptions behind it. For Product Representation Controls, advertiser research, product strategy, engineering behaviour, and go-to-market framing all had to tell the same story: what control advertisers had, what it changed, and how they could trust it. Bringing design closer to those decisions helped turn a technically complex product into something advertisers could understand, trust, and use with confidence.
