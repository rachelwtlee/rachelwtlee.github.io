0 → 1 foundational infrastructure giving advertisers direct control over how their product data appears in ads, for every objective.

Meta's product catalog ↗is the engine behind ads and commerce on Facebook and Instagram. Advertisers upload their products, with information like titles, images, prices, and Meta uses that data to power the ads and storefronts buyers see.

Most sophisticated advertisers use multiple data sources, like a Shopify connection, a data feed, to keep that data accurate. When two sources disagree on the same attribute, Meta decides which one wins. Before this project, advertisers had no visibility into that decision and no way to change it.

This problem affected two cohorts differently. The first needed one consistent version of each product but had no control over which source won when they conflicted. The second needed products to look different depending on the objective, but their only option was maintaining a separate catalog per objective, which was more overhead, and worse for ads performance.

Both groups wanted control over how their products appeared to buyers, but neither had it.

Phase 1: Configure Sources

Configure Sources gave advertisers what they were missing: visibility into which source was winning for each attribute, and the controls to change it.

I co-led foundational research with our researcher and scoping with engineering, then led design through to launch. Some highlights:

Discover what's in conflict, then change who wins

Attributes where sources are setting different values are surfaced first, ordered by how consequential they are. For example, a price conflict matters more than a description conflict. From there, advertisers drag to reprioritise, changing which source wins for any attribute.

Meta's default made visible (and overridable)

For attributes like price and stock, where wrong information has the highest downstream impact, Meta's automatically chosen source is clear. Advertisers can override it, but at least one source must always be set to ensure buyers always see consistent information.

Configure Sources: Outcomes

Configure Sources exceeded every goal. It exceeded the revenue protection goal by 2x, unblocked advertisers who couldn't run product ads without source-level control, and saved years of engineering time previously spent manually resolving data conflicts.

"The greatest product update from Meta I’ve seen all year." — Director of Marketing, Beauty Group

Phase 2: Product Data Versions

Configure Sources solved the first problem. But advertisers who needed products to look different across objectives still had no solution. Product Data Versions closed that gap by giving advertisers a way to create alternate versions of their product information within a single catalog, without changing the default.

I was the sole designer across both phases, leading design from alpha to beta. Beyond design execution, I co-authored a technical behaviour proposal with engineering to establish the right data model and the right mental model for advertisers before build began, and built a design vision grounded in what advertisers told us wasn't working. Together they became the roadmap for five engineering teams. I also owned the product strategy that got those teams to a shared understanding of the problem and what each needed to build to make the product work. Some highlights:

Create a version without changing the default

Advertisers choose which data sources power the version, so products can look different for a specific objective without affecting how they appear anywhere else.

Validate products before running ads

Advertisers can validate that version data uploaded correctly, that version information is applied to the right products, and that the ad looks right before anything goes live.

Product Data Versions: Outcomes

Product Data Versions launched to beta with strong advertiser satisfaction and measurable ROAS improvements1 reported across exit interviews, validating advertiser appetite and securing investment for General Availability. It also unlocked significant opportunity for growth initiatives that now depend on this infrastructure.

Key Takeaway

Design's job isn't just to make things look right. It's to make sure the thinking behind the product is as considered as the product itself.
