Collaborative Ads Brand Onboarding

A two-phase redesign where earning retailer trust through friction fixes made a larger structural change possible.

Key contributions

  • Redirected Phase 1 before build began. Ran a UX audit, reframed scope around the highest-friction gaps, and got leadership aligned to change direction
  • Consolidated seven independently scoped initiatives into a single workstream and secured investment for Phase 2 through Design and Product Reviews
  • Defined the vision for self-serve Collaborative Ads onboarding and co-developed the strategy with PM and engineering
  • Co-presented onboarding concepts directly to retailer partners and used partner feedback to reshape the implementation strategy
  • Led design execution across onboarding improvements and the new self-serve experience

Role

Lead Product Designer

Team

1.25 CD, 1 PM, 9 Eng, 1 DS, 1 PMM

Timeframe

11 months

Collaborative Ads ↗ lets brands run ads that direct buyers to their product pages on a retailer's website or app. Three parties are involved: brands run the ads, retailers host the catalog and process the sale, Meta connects them. Before a brand can run any ads, a retailer has to send them a collaboration request, and that process was broken in ways neither side could easily fix on their own.

The deeper problem wasn't just friction. Onboarding was structurally dependent on retailer workflows: retailers initiated requests, managed reviews, and absorbed the operational cost of every new brand they onboarded. More brands meant more retailer work, which naturally incentivised partners to gate access and prioritise their largest advertisers. Most brands discovered the product only when a retailer personally reached out, waited weeks for manual review, and still weren't set up to run their first ad by the end of it.

Retailers had spent years working around a product that hadn't kept pace with their needs. Before asking them to support a more ambitious onboarding model, we needed to demonstrate that we understood the problems they faced every day, and were willing to invest in fixing them. Phase 1 focused on rebuilding that confidence. Phase 2 used it to build something more ambitious.

Why onboarding mattered

Collaborative Ads had limited opportunities for product-led growth. Most performance improvements depended on broader ads infrastructure outside the team's control. Onboarding was different: increasing the number of brands using Collaborative Ads was the highest-leverage growth opportunity the team could directly influence, and new brand acquisition had been slowing for two years. That made onboarding the focal point for investment.

Retailers had spent years working around a product that hadn't kept pace with their needs. Before asking them to support a more ambitious onboarding model, we needed to demonstrate that we understood the problems they faced every day and were willing to invest in fixing them. Phase 1 focused on rebuilding that confidence. Phase 2 used it to make a structural change.

Phase 1: Friction fixes

I joined the team when a solution was already scoped. After running a UX audit and reviewing research, I paused the build, reframed the scope around the gaps that were actually breaking the experience, and got leadership aligned to change direction before any code was written. We shipped improvements to collaboration term enforcement, permissions assignment, and notifications.

Collaboration terms enforced end-to-end

Automate where safe, hold where it matters

Not all permissions carry the same risk. Ad account permissions, which involve access to spend and billing, stay manual. Catalog segment permissions, which let a brand's team use retailer products in ads, are auto-granted to anyone with ad account access.1 The result is that the people responsible for running ads aren't blocked from accessing products the moment setup completes.

Results

Outcomes

  • Onboarding completion rate increased by 13%
  • Reduced operational friction for retailers managing collaborations
  • Contributed to growth in new brands onboarding alongside launch go-to-market efforts

What it unlocked

  • Retailers began actively promoting Collaborative Ads to brands selling with them
  • Created the trust required to pursue self-serve onboarding
  • Aligned product, GTM, and partner teams around onboarding as the primary growth lever
Phase 2: Brand initiated onboarding

With retailer trust established, I led the vision to restructure onboarding as brand-initiated. Initial concepts required retailer engineering resources and retailer-owned infrastructure to implement. Through partner conversations, we learned that retailers loved the direction, but couldn't commit what the original approach required. That insight shifted the strategy: rather than building an onboarding model that depended on systems outside our control, we redesigned around surfaces Meta owned. Retailers maintained oversight over brand onboarding, but the operational burden was removed.

The final model introduced two paths: a fully self-serve flow for brands whose retailers had launched the model, and a structured collaboration request flow for everyone else.

One entrypoint for all brands

Not every retailer could support self-serve onboarding from day one, as making self-serve onboarding available requires bespoke engineering work per retailer. Rather than building separate entry points for different retailer paths, we designed a single form that routes based on retailer selection: self-serve where available, collaboration request where it isn't. The same entry point works for brands or anyone acting on the brand's behalf, including agencies, who manage the majority of Collaborative Ads campaigns.

Real-time verification

Shop URL verification gave brands a way to prove their relationship with a retailer without requiring retailer involvement, turning a multi-week manual process into something that could happen in a single session. Every brand already has a shop URL, so nothing extra is needed to get started. The approach required a deliberate security tradeoff: we implemented compensating mitigations before shipping to ensure it could be done safely at scale.

A direct path to the first ad

36% of brands who completed onboarding never ran an ad. One hypothesis was that there was no clear path to ad creation after setup, the obvious next step. The Overview tab tests that hypothesis: a direct entry point to run a Collaborative Ads campaign with defaults applied, alongside any remaining setup actions, so the path forward is visible the moment setup is done.

Results

Outcomes

  • Self-serve onboarding launched with one major retailer partner, with two more implementing the model, together representing a significant share of product revenue
  • Exceeded the daily revenue goal by ~37%
  • Onboarded thousands of new brands in the first weeks post-launch

What it unlocked

  • Gave retailers a reason to open Collaborative Ads to more brands, as onboarding no longer required proportional effort on their side
  • Our largest retailer made Collaborative Ads available to every brand on their platform for the first time, something the manual overhead had previously made unworkable
  • Created a path for brands to onboard and start advertising without needing retailer involvement
  • Set up the next phase of investment in brand activation and self-serve growth
Key Takeaway

Phase 1 wasn't just about fixing friction — it was the foundation that made a structural ask possible. Our top retailers extended Collaborative Ads to every brand on their platform because we'd already shown we understood their workflow. Trust isn't a soft outcome, it determines what you can build next.
