A two-phase redesign where earning retailer trust through friction fixes made a larger structural change possible.

Collaborative Ads ↗ lets brands run ads that direct buyers to their product pages on a retailer's website or app. Three parties are involved: brands run the ads, retailers host the catalog and process the sale, Meta connects them. Before a brand can run any ads, a retailer has to send them a collaboration request, and that process was broken in ways neither side could easily fix on their own. Most brands discovered the product only when a retailer personally reached out, waited weeks for manual review, and still weren't set up to run their first ad by the end of it.

This was a two-phase project. Phase 1 resolved key pieces of friction breaking the existing process. Phase 2 reimagined onboarding by removing structural bottlenecks, giving brands the ability to onboard themselves for the first time.

This project is under NDA. Screens shown here have shipped publicly. Full case study is available on request.

Phase 1: Friction fixes

I joined the team when a solution was already scoped. After running a UX audit and reviewing research, I paused the build, reframed the scope around the gaps that were actually breaking the experience, and got leadership aligned to change direction before any code was written. We shipped improvements to notifications, permissions assignment, and collaboration term enforcement. A few highlights:

Collaboration terms enforced end-to-end

Previously, retailers had no way to enforce their own collaboration requirements: they'd send an invitation to a brand and manually follow up to verify compliance. Terms are now configurable when they create a collaboration invitation.

When brands accept the invitation, terms surface as conditions of acceptance. 

Automate where safe, hold where it matters

Ad account permissions assignment stays manual because they involve access to spend and billing.

Catalog segment permissions, which let a brand's team use retailer products in ads, are auto-granted to anyone with ad account access, so the people running ads aren't blocked from showcasing products during ad creation.1

Phase 1 Outcomes

Phase 1 meaningfully exceeded its onboarding completion goal. Retailers responded by investing more in promoting Collaborative Ads to new brands — a direct result of the reduced friction and the trust built along the way.

Phase 2: Brand initiated onboarding

With retailer trust established, I led the vision to restructure onboarding as brand-initiated. I validated the concept with key retailer partners,2 worked through the constraints their feedback surfaced, and designed a fully self-serve experience from discovery to first ad. The same entry point also routes brands to a collaboration request flow where self-serve isn't yet available. A few highlights:

One entrypoint for all brands

Brands select their retailer upfront, and the form adapts from there. Routes to either a self-serve flow or a collaboration request depending on the retailer. The same entry point works for brands, or anyone acting on the brand's behalf.

Real-time verification

As brands type their Shop URL, Meta validates the collaboration instantly. If there's a match, brands can continue setup, removing back and forth with the retailer. Every brand has a Shop URL, so nothing extra is needed to get started.

A direct path to the first ad

Once setup is complete, brands land on a new Overview tab with a direct entry point to run a Collaborative Ads campaign with defaults applied. Next steps surface pending actions and best practices so nothing gets missed.

Phase 2: Outcomes

Phase 2 significantly exceeded its daily revenue goal at launch and onboarded thousands of brands in the first weeks. Our largest retailer partner removed eligibility gating entirely, making Collaborative Ads available to every brand selling on their platform for the first time.

Key Takeaway

Big structural change requires trust to be earned. Phase 1 wasn't just about resolving friction, it was the foundation that made a larger ask possible.
