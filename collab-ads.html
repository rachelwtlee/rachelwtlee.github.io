Collaborative Ads Brand Onboarding & Activation

A two-phase redesign that reduced retailer overhead, rebuilt confidence in the product, and shifted onboarding from retailer-led to brand-initiated.

Key contributions

  • Evaluated the existing Phase 1 direction, reframed how key friction points were addressed based on retailer and brand workflows, and secured leadership buy-in before implementation.
  • Unified 7 separately scoped roadmap projects into one workstream, giving the team end-to-end visibility across the problem space and revealing that one initiative was already solved by the proposed direction.
  • Led the vision for a brand-initiated onboarding and activation experience and shaped the strategy with PM and Engineering.
  • Co-presented concepts to retailer partners and used their feedback to reshape the implementation strategy.

Role

Lead Product Designer

Team

1.25 CD, 1 PM, 9 Eng, 1 DS, 1 PMM

Timeframe

11 months

Collaborative Ads ↗ lets brands run ads that direct buyers to their products sold through retailer websites and apps. Brands run the ads, retailers own the catalog and fulfil the sale, and Meta connects them.

Before a brand could advertise, a retailer had to send them a collaboration invitation. The process was broken in ways neither side could easily fix on their own.

The deeper problem wasn't just friction. Onboarding was highly dependent on retailers: retailers initiated requests, managed reviews, and absorbed the operational cost of every new brand they onboarded. More brands meant more retailer work, which incentivised retailers to gate access and prioritise their largest advertisers. Most brands discovered the product only when a retailer reached out, waited weeks for manual review, and still weren't ready to run ads by the end of it.

Phase 1 reduced retailer overhead and rebuilt confidence in Collaborative Ads. Phase 2 shifted onboarding from a retailer-managed process to a brand-initiated one.

Phase 1: Reducing retailer overhead

When I joined the team, a direction had already been scoped. Before committing to implementation, I audited the retailer and brand experience, reviewed partner feedback, and formed my own view of where the biggest onboarding constraints were. I got leadership and team buy-in to revise the approach before build began.

While the friction points were already known, I identified which were worth prioritising in this phase and reshaped how we addressed them. We focused on the changes that would have the greatest impact on retailer and brand workflows, while staying within the constraints of the product and timeline.

Retailers had collaboration requirements but no way to enforce them— brands sent screenshots to prove compliance and waited for manual verification. 80% of brands didn't know an invitation was waiting because we never notified them. Permissions were treated as a secondary step, even though assigning access to both the ad account and catalog segment determined whether a brand could run ads at all.

Together, they turned onboarding into a manual, time-consuming process for retailers and left brands waiting weeks to complete setup.

Collaboration terms enforced end-to-end

No more manual invitation follow-ups

Automate where safe, hold where it matters

Ad account permissions, which give teams access to spend and billing, remain manually assigned. Catalog segment permissions, which give access to products, are auto-granted to anyone with ad account access.1

Results

  • Onboarding completion rate increased by 13%
  • Retailers increased investment in Collaborative Ads and actively promoted it to brands
Phase 2: From retailer-led to brand-initiated

Phase 1 reduced retailer overhead and rebuilt confidence in the product. With the foundations in place, a harder problem remained: brands could only start using Collaborative Ads if a retailer sent them an invitation.

We knew reducing onboarding friction alone wouldn't be enough. I led the vision for an end-to-end experience spanning discovery, onboarding, and activation, bringing Collaborative Ads to the surfaces brands already used and reducing retailer involvement throughout the process.

We pitched the concept to our largest retailer partners. All liked the idea, but only one could commit. The others lacked the resources, couldn't meet our timeline, or hadn't launched their own brand portals yet.

That forced a strategic pivot. Rather than relying on retailer-owned surfaces, we shifted the experience to Collaboration Center, Meta's hub for retailer-brand collaborations. It had significantly less traffic, but gave us full control and the ability to scale independently.

One entrypoint for all brands

Brands shouldn't need to understand which onboarding model a retailer supports. Rather than creating separate entry points, we designed a single form that routes based on retailer selection: self-serve where available, a guided collaboration request where it isn't.

Real-time verification

Shop URL verification gave brands a way to prove their relationship with a retailer without requiring retailer involvement, turning a multi-week manual process into something that could happen in a single session. Every brand already has a shop URL, so nothing extra is needed to get started. The approach required a deliberate security tradeoff: we implemented compensating mitigations before shipping to ensure it could be done safely at scale.

A direct path to the first ad

36% of brands who completed onboarding never ran an ad. One hypothesis was that there was no clear path to ad creation after setup. The Overview tab tests that hypothesis by providing a direct entry point to launch a Collaborative Ads campaign with defaults applied, alongside any remaining setup actions.

Results

  • Our largest retailer made Collaborative Ads available to every brand on their platform for the first time.
  • Exceeded the daily revenue goal by ~37%.
  • Onboarded thousands of new brands in the first weeks post-launch.
  • Self-serve onboarding launched with our largest retailer partner, with four more in implementation.
Key Takeaway

Phase 1 wasn't just about fixing friction, it created the conditions for a larger structural change. Our largest retailer made Collaborative Ads available to every brand on their platform because we'd already shown we understood their workflow and constraints. Trust isn't a soft outcome; it determines what you can build next.
